Mechanism of Action

All AAS are derivatives of testosterone modified to alter the balance of anabolic and androgenic effects. They bind to intracellular androgen receptors (AR) with varying affinity. The AR-ligand complex translocates to the nucleus and regulates transcription of genes involved in myogenesis, resulting in increased muscle fiber cross-sectional area (particularly type II fast-twitch fibers), enhanced satellite cell activation, and reduced myostatin expression. Nitrogen retention improves, creating a positive nitrogen balance essential for protein synthesis. Erythropoietin stimulation increases red blood cell mass, improving oxygen delivery to working muscle. Different esters and 17α-alkyl modifications alter bioavailability, half-life, and hepatic metabolism.

Compound-Specific Notes

Testosterone Enanthate / Cypionate

The reference standard. Aromatizes to estradiol, requiring estrogen management at supraphysiologic doses. Esters extend half-life for weekly dosing. Used in hormone replacement therapy (50–100 mg/wk) and performance contexts (200–600 mg/wk).

Nandrolone Decanoate (Deca-Durabolin)

Favorable anabolic:androgenic ratio (125:37). Lower androgenic side effects than testosterone. Weakly aromatizes — estrogenic side effects are possible but less frequent. Long half-life (15 days) enables less frequent injection. Commonly stacked with testosterone. Potential for prolactin-related side effects at high doses.

Trenbolone Acetate

A potent 19-nor androgen with very high AR affinity (5x testosterone). Does not aromatize to estrogen. Binds strongly to progesterone and androgen receptors. Rapid action via acetate ester. Associated with significantly increased nitrogen retention, IGF-1 production, and nutrient partitioning. Short half-life requires frequent (EOD) injection. Not FDA-approved for human use; used in veterinary medicine as a growth promoter in cattle.

Methandrostenolone (Dianabol)

Oral 17α-alkylated AAS. Rapid onset of effects. Aromatizes to a potent estrogen, often requiring aromatase inhibitor use. Significant hepatotoxicity potential due to 17α-alkylation — liver enzyme elevation is common. Associated with rapid gains in muscle mass and strength over 4–6 week cycles. Half-life of 4–6 hours necessitates multiple daily doses.

Oxandrolone (Anavar)

Oral AAS with very favorable anabolic:androgenic ratio (320:24). Minimal hepatotoxicity compared to other oral AAS — FDA-approved for muscle wasting conditions (HIV, severe burns). Low androgenic side effect profile makes it suitable for female and adolescent patients under medical supervision. Does not aromatize. Strength gains are modest (10–15%) compared to injectable compounds, but with a favorable side effect profile when used at therapeutic doses (10–20 mg/day).

Stanozolol (Winstrol)

17α-alkylated AAS available in oral and injectable forms. Does not aromatize. Hepatotoxic with prolonged use. Low estrogenic activity makes it useful for cutting phases. Negative impact on HDL cholesterol is well documented (HDL reduction of 40–60% at 6 mg/day). Contraindicated in patients with hyperlipidemia or cardiovascular disease. Increases collagen synthesis and has been used for tendon and ligament health in some contexts.

Boldenone Undecylenate (Equipoise)

Testosterone derivative with a double bond at C1,2 conferring reduced androgenic activity. Weak aromatizer. Very long half-life (14 days) due to undecylenate ester. Originally developed for veterinary use. Increases appetite in some users. Mild compound with slow but steady gains. Less suppressive of endogenous testosterone than more potent AAS.

Methenolone (Primobolan)

Dihydrotestosterone (DHT) derivative. Low anabolic and androgenic potency. Does not aromatize. Available as oral acetate and injectable enanthate. Extremely mild side effect profile — considered one of the safest AAS compounds. Mild gains in lean mass with minimal water retention. Hepatotoxicity is minimal for the injectable form. Widely faked in underground markets.

Contraindications

  • Known or suspected prostate or breast carcinoma (androgen-sensitive)
  • Severe hepatic impairment (particularly for oral 17α-alkylated compounds)
  • Nephrotic syndrome or severe renal impairment
  • Hypercalcemia (androgens may increase serum calcium)
  • Pregnancy and lactation
  • Severe congestive heart failure (fluid retention exacerbation)
  • Untreated severe obstructive sleep apnea
  • Polycythemia (hematocrit >54%)
  • Known hypersensitivity to the compound or ester
  • Pediatric patients (risk of premature epiphyseal closure)

Overdose

Acute testosterone toxicity is rare due to the high LD50 in animal models (>2,000 mg/kg). Single-dose overdoses may cause nausea, vomiting, headache, and agitation. Chronic supraphysiologic use is associated with the adverse effects described above. There is no specific antidote; treatment is supportive and symptomatic. Activated charcoal is not effective for parenteral administration. Seek medical attention if overdose is suspected.

Considerations

All AAS are classified as Schedule III controlled substances in the United States except oxandrolone (Schedule II in some formulations). Use without a prescription is illegal. Documented adverse effects include suppression of endogenous hormone production (HPTA), gynecomastia (estrogenic compounds), testicular atrophy, azoospermia, acne, androgenic alopecia in predisposed individuals, increased hematocrit, and potential exacerbation of sleep apnea. Oral 17α-alkylated compounds carry significant hepatotoxic risk. Dyslipidemia (reduced HDL, increased LDL) is compound-specific but well documented. Left ventricular hypertrophy has been observed in long-term supraphysiologic use. Cardiovascular risk remains debated with conflicting evidence — current literature suggests risk is elevated in the presence of other risk factors (obesity, hypertension, dyslipidemia). Post-cycle therapy (PCT) with SERMs (tamoxifen, clomiphene) is commonly employed to restore endogenous testosterone production after cessation.
